What This Month's Data Is Telling Us
Skin Quality Has Entered the Picture
For the first time across four months, skin quality has risen to joint top concern alongside volume loss. Karen, this is worth noting — it suggests a segment of your summer audience is thinking about skin texture, tone and radiance rather than injectable treatments. Skin boosters, peels and microneedling content could be timely right now.
Value for Money Is Back Up
Value for money has jumped from 6 interactions in June to joint top priority this month alongside longevity. This is a pattern worth watching — summer visitors may be more price-conscious. It doesn't mean discounting, but it does suggest that content which clearly communicates what clients get for their investment would land well right now.
Your Core Audience Is Still Showing Up
Even in a quieter month, the 50s+ age group remains the dominant demographic — consistent across all four reports. This is now one of the clearest patterns in your data. Whatever brings them to your site is working consistently, and it's worth continuing to speak directly to this audience in your content.
The Quiz Is Still Being Seen
210 impressions and 188 button clicks this month tells us that despite the summer slowdown in completions, the quiz is still appearing in front of a significant number of visitors. The drop is in follow-through, not visibility. When activity picks up in autumn, those impression numbers should translate into stronger completion figures again.
Marketing Recommendations — What the Data Suggests You Do Next
Karen, four months of data gives us enough to move beyond observation and into action. Below are four practical content suggestions based specifically on what your quiz visitors have been telling us — each one matched to the channels available to you.
Blog
Write about skin quality this month
Skin quality has appeared in your top concerns for the first time this summer, while volume loss has been consistently high across three months. A blog post covering skin boosters, Profhilo, or microneedling — framed around summer skin and preparing skin for autumn — would speak directly to what your current audience is researching. Title suggestion: "Is Summer Taking a Toll on Your Skin? Here's What We Recommend."
Newsletter
Address the "value for money" mindset head-on
Value for money has risen as a priority this month. Your next newsletter could include a section that explains clearly what clients receive across a typical treatment journey — consultation, aftercare, review appointments — without discounting. Framing this as "what your investment includes" rather than a price list tends to reassure quality-led clients and convert the hesitant ones.
Newsletter
Write directly to your 50s+ audience
Your over-50s demographic has been your most consistent audience across every single month. Consider a newsletter section — or even a dedicated send — that speaks specifically to concerns common in this age group: volume loss, contouring, longevity of results, and looking refreshed rather than "done." These are all things your quiz data confirms this audience cares about. You don't need social media to reach them — they're already finding you.
Google My Business
Post about your autumn availability now
September and October are traditionally the strongest months for aesthetic treatment bookings as people return from summer with renewed focus on their appearance. A Google My Business post in late July or early August — something simple like "Autumn appointments are now open" with a booking link — plants the seed at exactly the right moment. First-time visitors have been a consistent part of your audience, and they often need a gentle prompt to take the next step.
A note on the figures: Google Analytics records every individual interaction with the quiz, so if a visitor engaged across multiple sessions or revisited questions, each tap registers separately. The impressions figure reflects how many times the quiz was displayed on your site — this will always be higher than quiz starts, as many visitors scroll past without engaging. The figures that count unique individuals — completions (5 unique users) and starts (13 unique users) — are the cleanest and most meaningful numbers in this report.
